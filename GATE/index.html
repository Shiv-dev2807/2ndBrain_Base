Mission Briefing

Read this once. It's the whole strategy in four moves.

01 Stack, don't split

You are not running two prep tracks. You're running one deep-CS campaign with two scoreboards. Every hour on DSA, OS, CN, DBMS, and COA pays both the GATE examiner and the interviewer. That's ~80% of the syllabus doing double duty. See The Convergence.

02 Peak on purpose

Champions don't run at 100% every week for 7 months — they periodize. Foundation → Completion → Consolidation → Sharpening. Syllabus complete by mid-October (same date GO Classes builds their own batch around), so November–January is pure sharpening, not scrambling.

03 Backward-plan the internship

"Deadline: 31 Dec" is not "start applying 31 Dec." Interview pipelines take 3–6 weeks. Portfolio + resume go live by early September. Applications ramp through September–November so offers can land by December.

04 Protect the machine

7 hours of sleep isn't rest, it's a performance setting — this is when consolidation into long-term memory happens. A brain running on 5 hours loses more processing speed than the extra study hour buys back. Every schedule below is built around this on purpose.

The Struggle Protocol still applies here. Every DSA problem gets a fixed timebox (30–40 min) of real, undistracted attempt before you touch a hint or editorial. Log it either way — solved or not — in your error log. The struggle is where the pattern gets wired in, not the solution.

03 · Weekday Protocol

College-Day Protocol

Ready-for-college at 8:00, class 9:00, home 4:30, walking commute both ways. ~6.5 hours of deep, structured work fit around a full college day — plus the walk and the classroom dead-time turned into passive gains.

TimeTrackBlock
Dead-time isn't dead. Walk to college = audio layer (GATE concept audio / your own recorded notes / a tech podcast). Every college break = a 5-minute flashcard pass on your phone (formulas, edge cases, syntax you keep forgetting). None of this is scheduled above because it's opportunistic — grab it when it appears.
04 · Full-Send Protocol

Non-College-Day Protocol

The full day is yours. ~12 hours of structured deep work, still with real breaks, real meals, and 7+ hours of sleep. This is the upper edge of what serious GATE toppers and competitive programmers actually report doing during peak prep — not a fantasy number.

TimeTrackBlock
Sector shift: once you're inside the Sharpening phase (from ~21 Dec), every second non-college day becomes a full-length timed GATE mock, run at the exact exam session timing. Mock-test alignment with real exam timing is a standard topper technique — it trains your body clock, not just your brain.
